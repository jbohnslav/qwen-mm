# Publish human-readable ARM/x86 D4 status report

## Goal

Publish a decision-oriented companion to the machine-generated D4 report that
explains, in plain language, the current ARM and x86 position, the major wins,
the remaining work, and the path to a releasable compact certification.

## Scope

- Preserve `result.json` and the generated `report.md` as canonical evidence.
- Add a separate readable report with architecture scorecards, representative
  speed and memory numbers, interpretation of the eight-thread efficiency
  gate, and a map from remaining work to Phase D follow-up tickets.
- Distinguish real optimization gaps from measurement-floor or host-noise
  issues without declaring either class passing.

## Non-goals

Do not change certification thresholds, recalculate or omit gates, relabel the
measured `MISS`, claim exhaustive/video/vLLM coverage, or launch new benchmark
compute.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [x] The report answers “where is ARM?” and “where is x86?” with separate,
      evidence-backed scorecards and representative measurements.
- [x] Major accomplishments and all five remaining workstreams are explained
      in natural language with their Kingdom ticket IDs.
- [x] The report explains why strong absolute t8 speedups can coexist with a
      failed parallel-efficiency gate and why some extreme memory ratios are
      measurement-floor dominated.
- [x] Every quantitative claim is traceable to the frozen certification JSON,
      and `make d4-validate` still passes unchanged.

## Worklog

### 2026-08-24 — architecture status extracted

The frozen result contains 122 gates: ARM passes 50/60 architecture-specific
gates, x86 passes 55/60, and both additional M4 point checks pass, for 107/122
overall. ARM's ten misses are six memory, two raw-RGB speed, and two image24/t8
efficiency gates. x86's five misses are two single-image memory, two
image24/t8 efficiency, and one slight official-baseline noise gate. All x86
speed gates pass; all encoded-image and selected batch speed gates pass on ARM.
